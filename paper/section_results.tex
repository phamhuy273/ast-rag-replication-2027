\subsection{Overall Benchmark Results across Twelve Systems}
\label{sec:results:benchmark}
Table~\ref{tab:main} presents the 12-configuration benchmark across lexical (BM25), dense bi-encoder (BGE-M3), and cross-encoder reranking tiers. 
At reranking, \lineH\ achieves the highest \text{NDCG@10} (0.621, 95\% CI $[0.555, 0.682]$). It ties \lineN\ on \text{NDCG@1} (0.800), while \lineN\ has a marginally higher \text{MRR@10} (0.953 vs. 0.950). \astH\ reaches 0.487 ($[0.419, 0.551]$), and \astN\ drops to 0.341 ($[0.263, 0.421]$).

Dense encoding improves NDCG@10 over BM25 by $0.118$--$0.139$ with headers, and reranking adds another $0.037$--$0.070$. 
Header gains differ across chunking strategies. For AST chunks, they grow with retriever capacity: negligible for BM25 ($+0.006$), moderate for dense retrieval ($+0.092$), and large for reranking ($+0.146$). For line windows, gains are modest at every stage ($+0.028$, $+0.030$, $+0.041$). 
Because pooling spanned all 12 systems at top-5, $\text{Judged@5}$ is $100.0\%$ for all configurations. 
Binary preference (\texttt{bpref}) orders the four conditions identically to NDCG@10 at every tier (Table~\ref{tab:main}). At the reranking tier: Line+H $0.353 >$ Line No-H $0.300 >$ AST+H $0.241 >$ AST No-H $0.170$. This indicates that rankings are robust to unjudged items at deeper ranks. Two patterns deserve mention: at the dense tier AST+Header attains higher NDCG@1 (0.620 vs. 0.580) and MRR@10 (0.910 vs. 0.883) than Line+Header, although it trails on NDCG@10 (0.450 vs. 0.551); reranking improves every condition over dense except AST No-Header, dropping from 0.358 to 0.341, consistent with cross-encoder reliance on header cues for isolated AST chunks.

\textbf{RQ1 (Context Headers):} Prepending headers yields a large, significant gain for AST chunks (\textbf{Comp 3}: $\Delta = +0.146$, 95\% CI $[+0.086, +0.207]$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} < 0.001$, $W = 24.0$; AST+H wins 23/25 JDs). In contrast, prepending headers to line windows yields a minor, non-significant gain (\textbf{Comp 4}: $\Delta = +0.041$, 95\% CI [$-0.012$, $+0.093$], $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 0.2411$, $W = 118.0$; 15 wins vs. 10 losses). An exploratory difference-in-differences test confirms that the header effect differs significantly between strategies ($W = 66.0, p = 0.0081$). As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:interaction_plot}, headers act as vital anchors for fine-grained AST chunks while providing diminishing returns for 50-line windows.

\textbf{RQ2 (AST vs. Line):} Without headers, RQ2 is tested by Comp 2 (confirmatory); with headers, by Comp 5 (exploratory). Comp 1 additionally compares across header conditions (AST+Header vs. Line No-Header). Simple line windows without headers significantly outperform AST chunks with headers (\textbf{Comp 1}: $\Delta = -0.093$, 95\% CI [$-0.164$, $-0.023$], $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 0.0438$, $W = 78.0$; Line wins 18 vs. AST wins 7). Without headers, line windows strongly outperform AST chunks (\textbf{Comp 2}: $\Delta = -0.239$, 95\% CI [$-0.292$, $-0.185$], $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} < 0.001$, $W = 3.0$; Line wins 23/25). In the exploratory contrast with headers (\textbf{Comp 5}), line windows remain significantly superior ($\Delta = -0.134$, 95\% CI [$-0.199$, $-0.070$], $p_{\mathrm{raw}} = 0.0003$, $W = 36.0$; Line wins 20 vs. AST wins 5).

\subsection{Subgroups, Morphology Sensitivity, and Header Effects}
\label{sec:results:sensitivity}
\textbf{Subgroups \& Common Support:} Trends hold across languages: Java ($N=15$) yields Line+H $0.716$ vs. AST+H $0.583$ ($\Delta = -0.133$); React/TS ($N=10$) yields $0.478$ vs. $0.342$ ($\Delta = -0.136$). 
AST fallback is $0.0\%$ (0/438 chunks, 0/250 top-10 candidates). 
Evaluating on \textit{Common Support} (826/882 pairs where both strategies have candidate chunks, retaining 91.8\% of relevant evidence across all 25 queries) confirms that Line+H retains its statistically significant lead over AST+H ($0.600$ vs. $0.489$, $\Delta = +0.111$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 0.0036$, $W = 51.0$; i.e., AST+H is lower by $-0.111$), while Line No-H dominates AST No-H ($0.535$ vs. $0.342$, $\Delta = +0.193$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} < 0.001$, $W = 11.0$). Restricting to common support removes only 17.3\% of the end-to-end gap (the gap shrinks by 0.023, $p = 0.0015$), demonstrating that 82.7\% of the gap is not explained by coverage.

\textbf{Window Overlap Sensitivity:} Suppressing intersecting 10-LOC overlapping windows from the same file in top-10 retrieval (26.4\% of Line top-10 slots involved in overlap [16.8\% redundant] vs. 0\% in AST [0.8\% in AST+Header due to nested units, Section~\ref{sec:approach:factorial}]) leaves deduplicated NDCG@10 intact: Line+H achieves $0.583$, Line No-H $0.574$, AST+H $0.482$, and AST No-H $0.341$. Line windows retain their significant lead (\textbf{Comp 5}: $\Delta = -0.101$, $p = 0.0013$; \textbf{Comp 1}: $\Delta = -0.092$, $p = 0.0236$), and the AST header boost remains large (\textbf{Comp 3}: $\Delta = +0.141$, $p < 0.001$).

\textbf{Qualitative Inspection of Header Lift:} Inspecting rank trajectories illustrates how headers ground isolated chunks. 
For \texttt{JD\_JAVA\_01} (\textit{"Spring Security \& JSON Web Token (JWT)"}), a chunk in \texttt{AccountController.java} (lines 39--64, \texttt{me()}, AST arm) that retrieves authenticated user context via \texttt{userService.getUser()} rises from Rank~6 without header to Rank~1 with header (human relevance 2). 
For \texttt{JD\_REACT\_01} (\textit{"React Admin Dashboard \& role-based access control (RBAC)"}), an administrative control panel in \texttt{Admin/index.tsx} (lines 544--613, AST arm) jumps from Rank~13 to Rank~1 with header (relevance 2).
