\section{Discussion and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Underlying Mechanisms and Scope}
Node-level AST extraction (without sibling merging as in \castname~\cite{zhang2025cast}) does not inherently surpass sliding windows in requirement evidence retrieval. 
Three mechanisms explain this: 
(1)~\textit{Information density and token budget}: 50-line windows (mean 41.0 LOC, 390.8 tokens) capture surrounding declarations, annotations, and helper calls, providing richer co-occurrence signals than isolated leaf AST nodes (mean 26.7 LOC, 250.8 tokens). This token gap is intrinsic to single-node extraction; without size-budgeted sibling merging to coalesce related leaf nodes, fine-grained AST slicing sacrifices contextual density. 
(2)~\textit{Contextual compensation}: headers inject the missing architectural namespace, allowing cross-encoders to match requirement keywords against file and class names. Notably, AST headers possess an asymmetric advantage by retaining class and method cues, yet line windows remain superior. 
(3)~\textit{Query granularity}: composite JD queries (5--8 skills) favor broader 50-LOC windows encompassing co-occurring functions over leaf nodes.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\textbf{Internal Validity:} Mitigated by 100\% pooling at top-5 across all 12 systems (100\% Judged@5, zero orphaned pairs) and blinded dual annotation ($\kappa_w = 0.9822$). Confounds from AST line coverage (69.0\% lines across 183/189 files; median 16 vs. 50 LOC; 15.8\% vs. 28.6\% exceeding 512 tokens), header metadata asymmetry, and window overlap (26.4\% of Line top-10 slots involved in overlap [16.8\% redundant] vs. 0\% in AST) were audited: common-support evaluation ($N=826$ shared pairs) confirms Line retains its lead ($\Delta = +0.111, p = 0.0036$), and deduplication preserves the ranking order.
\textbf{Statistical Conclusion Validity:} $N = 25$ queries provide 80\% power to detect paired differences in the MDE range of $\Delta \in [0.08, 0.13]$ across observed standard deviations ($\sigma_d \in [0.137, 0.184]$ across $\alpha \in [0.0125, 0.05]$); non-significant small differences (e.g., Comp 4, $\Delta = +0.041$) indicate absence of evidence rather than equivalence.
\textbf{Construct Validity:} Requirements were extracted from job postings via \texttt{gemini-1.5-flash} to reflect authentic industry expectations. Relevance was evaluated on an established 3-point ordinal scale. 
\textbf{External Validity:} Evaluated on Java Spring Boot and React/TypeScript with BGE-M3 and BGE-Reranker-Base; caution is warranted when generalizing to dynamically typed languages or distributed architectures.
