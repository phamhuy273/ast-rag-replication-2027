\section{Discussion and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Underlying Mechanisms and Scope}
Node-level AST extraction (without sibling merging as in \castname~\cite{zhang2025cast}) does not inherently surpass sliding windows in requirement evidence retrieval. 
Three mechanisms may explain this; we did not test them directly: 
(1)~\textit{Information density and token budget}: 50-line windows (mean 41.0 LOC, 390.8 tokens) capture surrounding declarations, annotations, and helper calls, providing richer co-occurrence signals than isolated AST nodes (mean 26.7 LOC, 250.8 tokens). This token gap is intrinsic to single-node extraction; without size-budgeted sibling merging to coalesce related nodes, fine-grained AST slicing sacrifices contextual density. 
(2)~\textit{Contextual compensation}: headers may inject missing architectural namespace, which may allow cross-encoders to match requirement keywords against file and class names. Notably, AST headers carry more metadata (class and method cues) than line headers, yet line windows remain superior. 
(3)~\textit{Query granularity}: composite JD queries (5--8 skills) may favor broader 50-LOC windows encompassing co-occurring functions over individual nodes.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\textbf{Internal Validity:} Mitigated by 100\% pooling at top-5 across all 12 systems (100\% Judged@5, zero orphaned pairs) and blinded dual annotation ($\kappa_w = 0.9822$). Confounds from AST line coverage (69.0\% lines across 183/189 files; median 16 vs. 50 LOC; 15.8\% vs. 28.6\% exceeding 512 tokens), header metadata asymmetry, and window overlap (26.4\% of Line top-10 slots involved in overlap [16.8\% redundant], vs. 0\% in AST No-Header and 0.8\% in AST+Header due to nested units) were audited: common-support evaluation ($N=826$ shared pairs) shows Line retains its lead ($\Delta = -0.111, p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 0.0036$), and deduplication preserves the ranking. The reranker truncates inputs at 512 tokens; 28.6\% of line windows and 15.8\% of AST chunks exceed this length before query prepending, so tail code of long chunks may not be scored. Pooling depth (5) is smaller than evaluation depth (10); unjudged items at ranks 6--10 receive zero gain, which may bias NDCG@10 downward (bpref yields identical condition rankings). The two headers carry different amounts of information (File and Lines for line windows; File, Class, Method, and Lines for AST chunks), so the header effect is confounded with header richness. The Tree-sitter grammars were extended before the final pooling and annotation round; no relevance judgments were collected with an earlier parser configuration.
\textbf{Statistical Conclusion Validity:} $N = 25$ queries provide 80\% power to detect paired differences in the minimum detectable effect (MDE) range of $\Delta \in [0.08, 0.13]$ across observed standard deviations ($\sigma_d \in [0.137, 0.184]$ across $\alpha \in [0.0125, 0.05]$); non-significant small differences (e.g., Comp 4, $\Delta = +0.041$) indicate absence of evidence rather than equivalence.
\textbf{Construct Validity:} Requirements were extracted from job postings via \texttt{gemini-1.5-flash} to reflect authentic industry expectations. Relevance was judged on a 3-point ordinal graded-relevance scale with task-specific definitions (Section~\ref{sec:approach:pooling}). 
\textbf{External Validity:} Evaluated on Java Spring Boot and React/TypeScript with BGE-M3 and BGE-Reranker-Base; caution is warranted when generalizing to dynamically typed languages or distributed architectures.
